Press **F5** to switch to the labelled **after-100-round experimental policy**; switching restarts the round. F5 returns to the prior policy. ## Exact requested experiment

Both roles learned independently for exactly **100 rounds through real Game::step on the desert map**: one AI controls the player, another controls one game bot. Original/final policies and every round are saved. Learning then stopped; the final game bot faced the player's original, frozen before-training policy for exactly **20 tests**.

**Result: 0 wins, 16 losses, 4 timeouts — 0% bot win rate.** This does not demonstrate improvement. Both models changing is not proof they became better. The new candidate is optional rather than silently replacing the prior policy. The separately trained player model is saved for research; it does not control a human player during normal gameplay.

The real roles have unequal weapon damage/cadence, as in the game; the result is role-specific, not a balanced model ranking. Earlier simplified and one-learner results are historical and are not this test. An earlier apparent 2%→16% gain was withdrawn after correcting a control mismatch. See the full [experiment report](https://github.com/tffere/tffere-strike/blob/main/training/runs/2026-11-08-real-dual/REPORT.md).
